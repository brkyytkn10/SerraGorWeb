Probiotic or Prebiotic? How to Feed Your Gut Flora

Category: Phytotherapy Author: Dyt. Serra Gör Date: November 6, 2026

One of the most common mix-ups I hear from clients is this: "I took a probiotic, but do I need a prebiotic too?" The two words sound alike but do different jobs. In short: probiotics are the live friends, prebiotics are their food. To feed your gut flora in balance, you need both.

What Is a Probiotic?

Probiotics are beneficial microorganisms that, when taken in sufficient amounts, live in your gut. Capsules and syrups are what many people think of first, but your kitchen already has plenty:

  • Kefir: Contains a wide variety of live cultures.
  • Yogurt: Choose one with live cultures and a plain ingredient list.
  • Homemade pickles and sauerkraut: Those fermented in brine. Vinegar pickles do not contain live cultures.
  • Shalgam, boza and homemade sourdough: Our traditional fermented foods.

What Is a Prebiotic?

Prebiotics are the fibres that feed the beneficial bacteria in your gut. They pass through the digestive system undigested, reach the colon and are used there by these bacteria. They are abundant in:

  • Oats and whole grains
  • Leeks, garlic, onions
  • Legumes: chickpeas, lentils, dried beans
  • Bananas (especially slightly underripe ones) and apples
  • Artichokes and asparagus
"Adding a probiotic is not enough on its own. The new arrivals need food too. Putting both on your plate on the same day is the simplest way."

Both Together: Simple Pairings

Taking probiotics and prebiotics together is called "synbiotic". You do not need a special product for it; pairings from your own kitchen are enough:

  • Kefir or yogurt + oats and banana
  • Yogurt + dips made with roasted onion and garlic
  • A lentil or chickpea dish + homemade pickles on the side
  • A leek dish + ayran on the side

Where to Start

While your gut gets used to this new balance, you may feel some gas or bloating for a few days. That is why I suggest starting small: one portion of a fermented food a day (a bowl of yogurt or a glass of kefir) and one portion of a fibre-rich food are enough. After a few weeks you can increase the amount.

About supplements: Taking a probiotic supplement is not necessary, and the same product does not suit everyone. If you are thinking of using one, it is best to decide the type and amount together with your dietitian or doctor. Starting with food is often a solid first step.
