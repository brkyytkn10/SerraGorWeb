Adaptogenic Herbs: Ashwagandha and Stress Management

Category: Phytotherapy Author: Dyt. Serra Gör Date: October 15, 2026

Waking up before the alarm, traffic, endless notifications, work piling up... The single biggest health threat in modern life may really come down to one thing: chronically elevated cortisol. Our bodies are built to handle short bursts of stress, but staying stuck in "alarm mode" directly affects weight gain, sleep quality and immunity. This is exactly where the group of plants phytotherapy calls "adaptogens" comes in.

What Is an Adaptogen?

Adaptogens are botanical agents, used in Ayurvedic and traditional medicine for thousands of years, that help balance the body's response to stress. Rather than suppressing a single hormone, they help regulate the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis so the body can decide more sensibly "when to go into alarm mode and when to calm down." Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is one of the most studied herbs in this group, and one I recommend most often in my practice.

What Does Ashwagandha Actually Do?

When working with clients, I especially consider ashwagandha in the following situations:

  • Cortisol balance: May help balance cortisol levels that rise under chronic stress.
  • Sleep quality: When the mind stays "switched on," adding ashwagandha to an evening routine can make falling asleep easier.
  • Energy and fatigue: May play a supportive role for clients experiencing burnout or constant tiredness upon waking.
  • Anxiety levels: Can be considered as part of a holistic approach during periods of elevated daily anxiety.
"Ashwagandha isn't a miracle pill — it's a support built on top of good nutrition, sleep habits and stress management, not a replacement for them."

Who Should Use It With Caution?

While ashwagandha is generally well tolerated, like any herb it isn't right for everyone. Don't use it without consulting your physician and dietitian first, especially if you:

  • Have a thyroid condition (ashwagandha can affect thyroid hormones)
  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Have an autoimmune condition (such as rheumatoid arthritis, lupus or Hashimoto's)
  • Take sedatives, thyroid medication, or immunosuppressant drugs
  • Have an upcoming surgery (it's recommended to stop at least 2 weeks beforehand)

Practical Tips

If it's determined to be a good fit for you, here's generally how we approach it:

  • Choose a standardized root extract form from a reputable brand
  • Consistent use for at least 6-8 weeks is needed to evaluate its effect — don't expect instant results
  • Taking it in the evening with food may reduce digestive discomfort
  • Tea form offers a milder effect; for more intensive support, a capsule/extract form may be considered

Remember: adaptogenic herbs don't replace balanced nutrition, regular sleep and movement — they complement them. If you'd like to build a personalized stress management plan, we can work through this together in our consultation sessions.
